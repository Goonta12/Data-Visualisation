Australia's Design Industries

History / People / Present Every logo, building and garment starts with a designer. Australian businesses are led by the designers therefore.

1. Design is a $61 billion industry

Design adds $61 billion a year. That is 3.4% of Australia's GDP. Two thirds comes from design services, architecture, software and interactive content. Designers in other industries add $6.4 billion.

2. A country of small businesses

In 2012, Australia had about 39,000 design businesses. New South Wales and Victoria hold two thirds. Most firms have no employees.

3. Where design is most common

Firms per 100,000 residents is a fairer count. Victoria and Western Australia lead in architecture. New South Wales leads advertising and other design. The Northern Territory has the fewest advertisers.

The same pattern showed in 2001.

Note: Counting methods changed so compared patterns only.

4. Where design is growing

South Australia's architecture firms grew 27%. Victoria's grew 15%. New South Wales stood still. Advertising shrank in Tasmania and the Northern Territory.

Only Victoria is both dense and fast-growing. 124 firms per 100,000 people, up 12%. New South Wales is dense but grew 6%.

5. Where design skills are trained

633,000 hold architecture or building qualifications. 401,000 hold creative arts qualifications. Creative arts includes design, music and performing arts. Both cluster around the capital cities.

Creative arts is 3.5% of qualifications nationally. In Sydney it reaches 6%. Over half live around Sydney and Melbourne.

6. Zooming in on the cities

Creative arts is strongest in inner suburbs. About 10.5% in Yarra and the Inner West.

Outer suburbs lean towards building instead. Creative share: Vincent 57%, Mandurah 22%.

7. The people behind the businesses

People doing design work rose from 350,000 to 460,000 between 2001 and 2007. Advertising overtook graphic design in 2007.

Advertising grew fastest, up 76%. Fashion design grew least.

Men did most graphic design work, but women's share rose from 35% to 46%. Fashion design stayed mostly female.

Women's share also rose in advertising. It fell in architecture.

Pay is where the gap is widest. In 2007, half of men earned $40,000+. Only 31% of women did.

8. Inside the industry

Design firms employ more than designers. In architecture, 71% hold design-related jobs. In advertising, only 31% do.

In short: a $61 billion industry, built from small businesses in the south-east. Skills are trained in inner cities. Women's share is rising, but pay lags. Most design data ends in 2012. Skills maps are from 2016.
