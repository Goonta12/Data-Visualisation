I. Design is a $61 billion industry

Design adds $61 billion a year. That is 3.4% of Australia's GDP. Two thirds comes from design services, architecture, software and interactive content. Designers in other industries add $6.4 billion.

The industry keeps adding jobs. Employment in architecture and design businesses rose from 55,840 in 2006 to 76,940 in 2016, up 38% in ten years. That is about 2,100 more people every year, or growth of 3.3% a year. Growth did not slow after 2011: both five-year periods added about 17%. The count includes everyone in design firms, from designers to managers and admin staff.

II. A country of small businesses

In 2012, Australia had about 39,000 design businesses. They cluster in the south-east. New South Wales and Victoria hold two thirds. The smaller states and territories hold far fewer. Colour shows each state's largest design industry. Most firms are tiny and have no employees. Drag the slider: NSW and Victoria have held about two thirds of firms since 2001.

Architects tell the same story. In 2011, about 1 in 7 worked alone, with no staff. Tasmania was highest, at more than 1 in 4. South Australia followed at 18%. The ACT and Northern Territory were lowest, near 9%. Over a third of architects ran their own practice. Drag back to 2001 and about 1 in 4 worked alone, with Tasmania highest at 44%.

III. Where design is most common

Firms per 100,000 residents is a fairer count. Victoria and Western Australia lead in architecture. New South Wales leads advertising and other design. The Northern Territory has the fewest advertisers.

The same pattern showed in 2001. Victoria and Western Australia already led on architecture, and New South Wales on advertising and other design. So the gaps between states are long-standing, not the result of one strong decade. Sydney's advertising lead likely reflects its role as the base of most large agencies and media companies.

Note: The ABS changed how it counted businesses between 2001 and 2012, so the exact numbers can't be compared directly. Compare which states rank high or low, not the size of the change.

IV. Where design is growing

South Australia's architecture firms grew 27%. Victoria's grew 15%. New South Wales stood still. Advertising shrank in Tasmania and the Northern Territory. Census counts of architects back this up: from 2001 to 2011, Victoria's rose 50%, while New South Wales's grew only 16%.

Only Victoria is both dense and fast-growing. 124 firms per 100,000 people, up 12%. New South Wales is dense but grew 6%. Together, these suggest Melbourne was catching up with Sydney as the country's design centre.

V. Where design skills are trained

633,000 hold architecture or building qualifications. 401,000 hold creative arts qualifications. Creative arts includes design, music and performing arts. Both cluster around the capital cities.

Creative arts is 3.5% of qualifications nationally. In Sydney it reaches 6%. Over half live around Sydney and Melbourne. Design skills follow the big cities, where universities, studios and clients are concentrated. Regional areas train far fewer creative graduates.

VI. Zooming in on the cities

Creative arts is strongest in inner suburbs. About 10.5% in Yarra and the Inner West. That is three times the national rate of 3.5%. These inner suburbs sit close to universities, galleries and the city's studios and agencies, which draws creative graduates to live there.

Outer suburbs lean towards building instead. Creative share: Vincent 57%, Mandurah 22%. Outer areas are where most new housing is built, so building and trade skills are in demand there. Creative skills cluster where creative jobs are: close to the city centre.

VII. The people behind the businesses

People doing design work rose from 350,000 to 460,000 between 2001 and 2007. That is a rise of about 31% in six years. Advertising overtook graphic design in 2007.

Advertising grew fastest, up 76%. Fashion design grew least. The mid-2000s brought the first wave of online advertising, creating new work in web and digital campaigns. Fashion design, by contrast, is a small field, and local clothing manufacturing was shrinking as production moved offshore. Design work grew where business demand grew.

Men did most graphic design work, but women's share rose from 35% to 46%. Fashion design stayed mostly female. Graphic design moved close to an even split in six years, while fashion is the one field where men remain the minority.

Women's share also rose in advertising. It fell in architecture. That runs against Census counts of architects, where women's share rose from about 20% in 2001 to 28% in 2011. The survey counts anyone doing architectural design work, paid or unpaid, so the two measure different groups. Either way, women remained a minority in architecture.

Pay is where the gap is widest. In 2007, half of men earned $40,000+. Only 31% of women did. Part of the gap may come from women being more likely to work part-time, but it means women's growing share of design work has not yet brought equal pay.

VIII. Summary of the industry

Design firms employ more than designers. In architecture, 71% hold design-related jobs. In advertising, only 31% do. The rest are managers, sales, clerical and support staff. Agencies need large account and media teams, so fewer of their staff are designers.

Who does the work

The latest figures give the fullest picture. About 125,300 people now work in the four main design occupation groups. Graphic and web design and illustration is the largest, with 56,100 people, 45% of the total.

Pay and gender

Architects are the best paid, at a median $2,308 a week full-time, and added the most jobs in the past year (+1,000). Interior design is the most female field (73%) and architecture the least (37%), while graphic and web design has reached an even split.

In Short

Conclusion

A $61 billion industry, built from small businesses in the South East region of Australia. Skills are trained in inner cities.

Big and growing. Design adds 3.4% of GDP, and jobs in architecture and design firms rose 38% in ten years, from 55,840 in 2006 to 76,940 in 2016. About 125,300 people now work in the main design occupations.

Small firms, mostly in two states. Around 39,000 design businesses operate, and about 6 in 10 have no employees. NSW and Victoria have held two thirds of them since 2001.

Victoria is rising. Per person, Victoria and WA lead in architecture and NSW in advertising. Victoria is the only state that is both dense and fast-growing, a sign Melbourne is catching up with Sydney.

Skills sit in the inner city. Creative arts is 3.5% of qualifications nationally but about 10.5% in suburbs like Yarra and the Inner West, while outer suburbs lean towards building trades.

More women, unequal pay. Women's share of graphic design rose from 35% to 46%, and graphic and web design is now an even split. But in 2007 half of men earned $40,000+ from design, against 31% of women.
